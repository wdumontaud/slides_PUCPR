The model

Domus simulates the building (one thermal zone per apartment); the heat through the studied façade is computed by a 2D wall model. They exchange data at every Domus time step.

id2d2l — two materials separated by a curved interface. The volume of layer 1 stays H·d1: only its distribution changes.
sh2d1l — one material; the curve is the shape of the external face.

Outputs (Simulation options → Outputs)

OutputGraphs
temperature fieldmovie of T
temperature and fluxes fieldmovies of T, qx, qy
functionals fieldflux into the zone and consumed work over time (W, over the whole studied façade)
Super time stepping (sh2d1l)

sh2d1l uses an explicit super time stepping scheme, chosen in Super-Time-Stepping: RKL1 (Runge-Kutta Legendre, order 1, default), RKG1 (Gegenbauer, order 1), RKL2, RKG2 (order 2).

Coupling with Domus

The wall model returns the mean inside surface temperature and the convection coefficient of the façade (one pair per floor for a façade over several floors).

Inside the model every boundary keeps its detail: convection along the height, sun on the sunny part only, long-wave with the scene strip by strip.

Pitched roofs

Each building gets an unheated attic zone. Roof slope (°) and Min. depth for two slopes (m) set the shape.

Ridge along the street (or the wing), gables at free ends, hips and valleys at 45° in the corners of a courtyard.

Details: compositions, corners, long-wave

CornersSame height: hip on the outer diagonal, valley on the inner one. A lower building against a taller one: its ridge runs up to the taller wall, or ends with a hip.

Direct sun on the facade

Facade sun chooses where the direct sun falls on each face of the façade:

Rays on the urban sceneReal shadows: from the side, under overhangs.
Sunny area of DomusShadow at the bottom of each face, on (1 − Asun) of its height (figure).

Rays on the urban scene: from points 10 cm apart on the façade (one face per floor and band), a ray goes towards the sun; a point is in the shade when the ray meets a building, a roof or the overhang of a window. A shadow from the side, or a taller building in front of part of the width, falls where it really is. In both cases each face receives the sun energy Domus gives it. Graphs → Facade sun shows it.

Approximations
  • Rays: the overhangs of the windows of the studied façade are taken, not those of the other façades; a face whose rays see no sun while Domus sees some takes the shadow at its bottom.
  • Sunny area of Domus: a shadow from the side is converted into the same area at the bottom.
  • Diffuse and reflected radiation are uniform over the height.

External long-wave of an urban scene

Each strip of the façade exchanges long-wave radiation with the sky, the ground and every surface of the scene it sees, weighted by its view factor.

View factors: exact 3D, hidden parts removed, printed in the terminal.

qlw(y) = − ε σ [ Fsky(Ts⁴ − Tsky⁴) + Fsol(Ts⁴ − Tsol⁴) + Σk Fk(Ts⁴ − Text,k⁴) ]

The surfaces most seen get their own temperature (Surface temperature inputs); the others take the temperature of a neighbour.

Approximations
  • Surfaces of the scene are black bodies at their temperature (no reflection between them).
  • One temperature per Domus face; windows of other buildings are treated as wall.
  • Sky isotropic, ground uniform.
  • sh2d1l: the strips follow the curved shape and the façade also sees itself (hollows).

Boundary conditions

By edge of the wall: External and Internal (convection and radiation), Bottom and Top (convection). Convection: Dirichlet, constant, space dependant, McAdams (external), corner model (internal); the bottom also offers a foundation on ground.

A Dirichlet mode imposes the surface temperature, so the radiation of that side is set to No radiation. The foundation needs a wall that reaches the ground.

Foundation on ground

The bottom of the wall rests on a concrete footing instead of seeing the sunlit ground surface. Nothing to fill in; meaningful only when Zone 1 is on the ground floor.

Two soil columns (outside, under the building) give the temperature at the base of the footing; the ground temperatures come from the weather file.

Numerical details

Footing 0.20 m / 1.75 W/m·K, soil 1.5 W/m·K and 2.0×10⁶ J/m³·K, slab 0.15 m²·K/W, columns 4 m deep with 25 nodes. The terminal prints them (block Foundation). Constant zero = h = 20 W/m²·K towards the ground surface.

Studied facade over the whole building height

Studied facade = Whole façade: one 2D wall over all the floors of the building of Zone 1 (at most 4), each floor coupled with its own apartment. Cost of the wall model: n times one floor.

The curve spans the whole height. All these apartments are studied zones: the optimisation sums their consumptions.

Details: inside, slabs, outside
InsideAir, convection and long-wave of the apartment of each floor.
Floor slabsWithin 10 cm of each floor the wall touches the slab (fin towards the two apartments).
OutsideSun of the face of each floor; long-wave over the whole height.
Bottom / topBottom mode on the ground floor; top adiabatic.

Vertical bands of the studied facade

Vertical bands cut the façade over its width: each band gets its own sun and shadow.

no window 1 – 51 window 1 · 3 · 52 windows 1 · 5

Cost of the wall model: about one more model per band.

With 1 band (default) each floor of the façade is one Domus wall with one sun. With more bands, a shadow that does not cross the façade horizontally (the corner of a building in front, a wing of the courtyard) is seen band by band. A window is always one band (window + 20 cm on each side); the plain wall shares the other bands, cut symmetrically. The numbers this façade cannot take are greyed in the form.

Tab Facade and the 3D view show the bands (yellow). Graphs: the fluxes of the façade are the mean of its bands; direct, band k in the legend shows one band.

Heating / cooling

Configure = Studied zone(s) or Other zones, each with its own Control:

ControlWhat Domus simulates
on/offelectric heater and split air conditioner, all-or-nothing: sizing, setpoint, hysteresis
PIDthe same systems, PID control
offno system, indoor temperature imposed: constant or outdoor law
thermal loadkept between two setpoints; Domus reports the load needed

Outdoor law (defaults): 19 °C while it is 17 °C or colder outside, 24 °C from 27 °C, a straight line in between; the value of each hour holds for the whole hour.

Power of the heater and of the air conditioner, field Sizing:

SizingPower of each zone
auto1.2 × its peak load, computed by Domus once per case (at least 10 W/m²; coldest week for heating, hottest week for cooling, with the plane wall), then reused
manualthe value in W/m² × the floor area of the zone (0 = no system)

Under the field: the power of each zone and the recommended power, with a warning when they are far apart.

The recommended power is the one of Size with Domus when the case was sized, else an estimate from the walls and windows (heating only). The warning appears below 0.8 × (the setpoint may not be held) or above 3 × the recommended power (short cycles). An optimisation sizes its base case once and keeps these powers for every design.

PID. Keep Ki = 0 and Kd = 0 for long runs. Gains are per m² of floor of each zone, so they follow the size of the zone. Ki: no anti-windup, after a warm spell the heater stays off for days. The control of the other zones matters for Zone 1: heat crosses the shared walls.

Windows

Windows inZone 1 only · Zone 1 + neighbours · Studied building (advised) · All zones. CPU cost: low → high.
Glazingsingle (U 5.94) or double (U 2.8), always closed.
On the studied facadeon: the same windows on the façade of the wall model (see below).

Windows on the studied facade

The façade is split into vertical strips; each kind of strip is one wall model (tab Facade).

Each apartment receives the mean of A and B over its wall, windows excluded; the glazing itself is simulated by Domus.

Approximations
  • No heat flows across the width: A and B do not exchange heat.
  • Sill, head and reveals are cut (no thermal bridge, no frame).
  • A column of windows sees the scene from its own place.

Interface / wall shape

The curve that the optimisation varies: the boundary between the two layers (id2d2l, Interface definition) or the external face (sh2d1l, Wall shape definition). The material volume always stays that of a flat wall of thickness d1.

TypeParametersCurve
splinep0 amplitude, p1 position of the bulgea cubic
bezierparticles: x of the ends, (x, y) of the middle onesattracted by the points
rbfthe same particles + a kernelpasses through every point
blocksgrid nx × ny, click a cell (id2d2l)staircase, any topology
level-setcoefficients ck (sliders) on a grid (id2d2l)smooth field → blocks

bezier and rbf particles

  • P1 is at the bottom, the last particle at the top: only their x is editable. + Add particle inserts one.
  • Example: 0.2,0.2,0.4,1,0,2 = points (0.2, 0), (0.4, 1), (0, 2), (0.2, H).
  • rbf kernels: multiquadric, inverse multiquadric, thin-plate spline (not for sh2d1l), Gaussian.

level-set

A smooth field is evaluated on each block; the blocks with the highest values become layer 1, as many as the volume needs. c = 0 (Zero) gives the flat interface; Random draws a valid design.

nbx, nbyBlocks along x and y.
Basis, mx, mycosine modes or Gaussian bumps (m = mx·my coefficients).
ampWeight of the perturbation: larger = more tortuous, more rejected designs.
Rejected designs: a block smaller than 4 mesh cells, or an empty layer. Islands are allowed.

Optimisation

Searches the interface (id2d2l) or the wall shape (sh2d1l) that uses the least energy in Zone 1. The rest of the form is the fixed case.

How it works

1. flat wall→2. Ns random designs→ 3. kriging model of kWh = f(design)→4. simulate the most promising→ back to 3 until Ntot

Invalid designs are rejected before any simulation. The field Method offers a second way: a genetic algorithm, which needs many more simulations (made for the cluster or for short periods).

The kriging model predicts the energy of any design and its uncertainty; the next designs simulated balance a low prediction and a large uncertainty. Variables: p0, p1 (spline), the particles (bezier, rbf, within the bounds of opt/krige/input/minmax, written for a wall 0.4 m thick and 3 m high and scaled to the thickness of the wall and the height of the studied façade), the coefficients (level-set).

Genetic algorithm (NSGA-II): a population of designs is simulated, the best ones are kept and crossed, generation after generation (individuals × generations simulations); on the cluster it can use several nodes.

Run, stop, resume

  • Run optimisation starts the run in its own process: closing the hub does not stop it. On an existing name: running → followed again; stopped → resumed; finished → raise Ntot (kriging) or the generations (genetic) to go on.
  • Stop once = clean stop after the simulations running; twice = kill now.
  • Regenerate IDF builds the run's own copy of the IDF; the IDF of the form is never touched.

Several runs at the same time

One chip per run above the terminal: click it for its terminal, progress and Stop. One local run at a time, any number on the cluster.

Closing the hub changes nothing: at the next start it follows the runs still alive again and fetches the cluster jobs that ended meanwhile.

Files of a run
  • opt/krige/runs/<name>/ (kriging) or opt/nsga2/runs/xdomf90/<name>/ (genetic): settings and form, log, every simulation, the kept Domus folders (best/).
  • Working copies in the working folder (temp_opti/<name>), deleted when the run is finished, kept while it is stopped.
  • Command line: python opt/krige/main.py status|stop|resume <name>.

Windows, Linux and the cluster

The same hub, model and optimiser everywhere. Domus is a Windows program: on Linux it runs under Wine, inside a container image.

What is Wine, the image, the screen?
WineNot an emulator: the Windows functions Domus calls, for Linux. Each Domus runs in its own prefix (a fake C:\).
The imagedomus-wine.sif: a small Ubuntu with Wine, a virtual screen and Python, run by apptainer without administrator rights.
A screenDomus counts sunlit pixels with OpenGL: the image brings a screen in memory, one per Domus.

Where are my files?

mod/xdomf90/idf/ ├── <name>.idf the Domus project, rewritten at each run └── #<name>/saidas/ what Domus keeps between runs (sunny areas, state) mod/xdomf90/runs/<name>/<run>/ one simulation: Domus database, wall model *.bin, its idf, run.json opt/krige/runs/xdomf90/<run>/ an optimisation (kriging): settings, log, every simulation, best/ opt/nsga2/runs/xdomf90/<run>/ an optimisation (genetic algorithm): the same, one file per generation hubgui/exports/ exported figures (PDF, SVG, CSV) Documents\TOPS results <date> results kept by the uninstaller

A simulation is named by its start and its case, e.g. 2026-10-06_2103_id2d2l_bezier5_Nice. In the list of the simulations (Graphs): Rename gives it a name in the hub, Export makes a .zip to send it (hubgui/exports/runs), Import adds a simulation sent by someone else (its folder or its .zip): its project is chosen (a new one, made from its idf, if this computer has none of the same geometry) and its form loaded.

Format of the .bin files

Two int32 (nx, ny, ny = 0 for vectors) then float64 values, column-major (one column per model time step; the fields T, fH, fV: one column per Domus time step, see fields.txt).
